# Who opens FINN

A portfolio manager or analyst on a lean public-markets team like ENAM's. Four or five people run the whole book. They hold 18 names across banks, industrials, consumer and pharma, and each one is a high-conviction position they will live with for years. They read primary documents themselves and distrust commentary. What they lack is time: 286 exchange filings landed on this book in the month to 28 October, and a few dozen of them mattered.

They judge a company largely by its management. The question under every other question is: when these people say something on a call, does it happen?

FINN is on their phone. They open it in four moments.

## 1. Before the open (7:30 to 9:15)

Coffee, the commute, the minutes before the morning meeting. The question: what changed overnight, and must I act on anything?

- **10 seconds:** how many things need me today, and which companies. Or that nothing does.
- **1 minute:** for each of those few things, what happened, in one line, with the number that matters (a result's four lines, who the new CFO is and where they came from, the size of a deal against revenue).
- **10 minutes:** open the one that worries them, read the filing, and check whether this management has a habit of missing.
- **Next:** raise it in the morning meeting, or note a question for the next call. Rarely, a trade.
- **What makes them close the app:** a feed with no end. Paperwork mixed in with results. Having to open a PDF to find the one number. Not knowing whether they have seen everything.

## 2. During market hours (9:15 to 15:30)

They are in meetings or on other screens. The question: did something just cross my line?

- **10 seconds:** a notification that names the company, what happened and the line it crossed ("Crompton fell 6.2%, past your 4% line").
- **1 minute:** is there a filing that explains it, or is the move unexplained? What the price did over the last month.
- **10 minutes:** rarely. Usually they move the line if it fires too often.
- **Next:** dismiss it, or keep it for the evening.
- **What makes them close the app:** alerts they can't explain or tune, and alerts that fire on routine noise.

## 3. After the close, and in results season

Results come out after 3:30 pm and late into the night. The question: what did they report, and did they do what they said?

- **10 seconds:** revenue, EBITDA, PBT and PAT, each up or down against last quarter and the same quarter last year.
- **1 minute:** set against what management promised for this period. Margins guided at 12% and delivered 11.2% is the story; the raw numbers alone aren't.
- **10 minutes:** the promise ledger: each promise from the last four calls, quoted with its page, graded against the filed numbers. The questions to ask on tomorrow's call.
- **Next:** dial into the call with those questions, or update their own notes.
- **What makes them close the app:** a summary of what management said, with no check against what happened. Any number they can't trace to a page.

## 4. When an idea comes up

A colleague mentions a company, or a name keeps appearing. The question: what is this company, and can I trust its management?

- **10 seconds:** the company's name and size with a one-line verdict on management's word ("Kept 3 of their last 5 dated promises").
- **1 minute:** the trajectory (six years of revenue and profit), whether profits turn into cash, and the tight case each way.
- **10 minutes:** every promise with its quote and evidence; debt and the working-capital cycle.
- **Next:** add it to a watchlist, or drop it.
- **What makes them close the app:** a wait with nothing to read. Broker-style prose. Anything that sounds like a buy or sell call.

## What they should feel at the end

In control of their book. They know what changed, what they can ignore, and which management teams keep their word. FINN gives no buy or sell calls and no price forecasts, and every number it shows comes from a filing. Confidence comes from clarity.
