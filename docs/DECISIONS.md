# Decisions

**Point of view (stage 4): FINN is a brief you finish. Each morning it hands you the few things that changed, holds each one against what management promised, and lets you go.**

Each entry: what I chose, what I passed on, and why.

## Interaction model (stage 4, 31 Oct)

**A brief you finish is the spine.** The investor opens FINN before the open, reads the few cards that need them, and reaches the blue band that ends it ("That's the last 24 hours."), with the routine filings folded below the cards. On the next visit, what is new since the last one is marked. I scored seven models against the investor's day in `docs/INVESTOR.md`; this one won on every axis because it ends. A feed never tells you you're done, and knowing you've seen everything is what saves the time. Full scoring in `design/STAGE4_EXPLORE.md`.

**Push lives at the top of the brief, as notifications.** Anything that crossed one of the investor's lines (a ±% day, a volume multiple, a kind of filing) arrives as a notification card with the line printed on it, and the line can be moved from there. FINN's data is a snapshot built after the close, so I don't claim a background push the pipeline doesn't do; with permission, opening the app raises a real system notification for what's new.

**Pull lives inside every item, as Ask.** Each item can be asked about in place ("Did they deliver on margins?"), and the answer comes back as promised against delivered, quoted with its page. Questions about promises are answered from the 22 cached ledgers with no model call, so Ask works when Gemini is out of quota. A company outside the 22 is read live only when the server has a model key, which the link doesn't.

**An alert's figure belongs to the event, not the window.** A move on results day arrives as a move in the 24-hour window and rides with its results in a wider one. It used to lead with the day's move in one and PAT YoY in the other, so the same Syngene day read +12.2% or −52% depending on the window. Now anything that moved the shares leads with the day's change everywhere (it is what crossed the line), the profit goes in the sentence, and results with no move lead with PAT YoY. The reader still opens results on the table.

**The tab bar stays over a card's full view.** The first reader covered the tabs on purpose: reading the brief was one task with one way out. When the reader became the card growing into its full view, the tabs stayed and Back became the way out; the brief still ends, on its band.

**Ask shows what's ready outside the book.** When an idea comes up, the investor's first question is whether FINN can talk about that company yet. Look up lists the four companies FINN has read beyond the portfolio under Ready to open, and its field hints at one it has read in full ("LT, or a question"), so the first thing a newcomer types opens a graded page. Reading a new company takes about a minute with a model key; on this link it is off, and Look up says so before any tap.

**Against their word starts with this year's guidance.** A results item used to show only the last promise that fell due, which for L&T was a FY26 miss everyone already knew about. This quarter counts toward FY27, so the section now opens on the guidance still running for this financial year, with the year so far beside it when both are about revenue ("FY27 guidance: Revenue growth 10–12%." and, under it, "H1 revenue grew 11.4% on a year ago, inside the range so far."). There's no verdict on it: half a year isn't the year. Segment margins and returns aren't put next to the consolidated table, because they measure something else.

**Passed on:** chat as the home screen (typing is the slowest way to ask a phone anything, and a blank box hands the filtering back to the investor); a dashboard of tiles (aggregation, which the brief says isn't the scarce skill); voice as the main mode (fine on a commute, useless in a meeting). Voice stays as a Listen control that reads the brief aloud with the browser's own speech.

## Product

**The organising idea is a promise ledger.** FINN keeps a record of what management said it would do on its earnings calls and marks each promise Done, Partly done, Missed, No update or Not yet due. Push and pull both hang off it: a results filing is shown against the promises it settles, and the research page is the ledger first and the numbers second.
Passed on: a feed with summaries (what MarketPing, Stox AI and Alpha Tribe already do), a chat box, a dashboard. The brief says the thing ENAM values most is "what was promised vs. what was actually delivered", so I made that the spine instead of one tab out of eight. Full ideation in `docs/ideation.md`.

**Push is mostly folding.** Of 286 filings across 18 holdings in the 30 days to 28 October, 43 can interrupt, 113 get one line and 130 are folded into a count. The tier policy is written at the top of `finn/classify.py` and `finn/triage.py` so it can be argued with.

**Size things against the company.** Any filing with a rupee amount in it (a tax demand, a deal, a debt raise) is read for that amount and divided by the company's trailing revenue. Under 0.5% it becomes a one-line note. A ₹27 cr GST notice at Blue Dart is noise; ₹10,000 cr of NCDs at Sun Pharma is not.

**Management changes are ranked by who moves.** CEO, MD, CFO and executive directors interrupt. Business heads and independent directors get a line. Company secretaries are folded.

**Bios come from the filing, not LinkedIn.** The brief suggests LinkedIn as an example. SEBI's disclosure rules make companies file a "brief profile" of every incoming director and KMP, so the company's own filing already has the background, and scraping LinkedIn breaks its terms. Trade-off: the profile is only as good as what the company writes.

**Unusual moves say whether a filing explains them.** A 5× volume day after results is expected; a 5× volume day with no filing is the one to look at. Thresholds (volume multiple, price move, which filing types can interrupt) are set by the investor in "Your lines" and change Today immediately.

**Promises need a number or a concrete deliverable and a time frame to be graded.** Everything else ("we remain confident of strong growth") is counted as talk and shown as a number, never graded. This came from the fresh critic's pre-mortem: a ledger full of vague items would be either empty or wrong.

**The book is sorted weakest word first.** It is the view a PM would want before a review: which management teams are drifting from what they said. Since 31 Oct the sort counts promises broken (missed or gone quiet; partly kept counts half), then the share kept: Home First, 1 of 7 kept, comes before Havells, 0 of 1. Two of three panel critics read a lone quiet promise at the top as noise, and the count is what a PM would act on.

## Grounding and trust

**One grade corrected by hand, and said so (31 Oct).** L&T's Q2 FY26 promise to close the Hyderabad Metro handover by the end of FY26 was graded "partly kept" on evidence about the Nabha Power sale, a different divestment. My own hand review on 28 Oct missed it; a critic's note about a vague promise line led me to the transcript. I set it to "can't tell", named the promise properly, and removed the same mistake from L&T's summary, one question and one point against. The entry carries a `corrected` note, and the grade review now says 17 of 20 hold, not 18. I didn't regrade the company, because a fresh model run would change grades nobody has checked.

**Two more corrected by hand: TCI's "this year" (31 Oct).** A critic asked which year "12 to 15% growth by this year" meant. TCI said it on its May 2025 call, the year-end call, so this year is FY26. The grader read it as FY25, and the quote (page 8) is about the supply chain business, not the company: it is now "can't tell", since that business's FY26 revenue isn't in the numbers read. The company guide on page 4, "10 to 12% growth on the top line and bottom line this year", had been left not due with a note about FY27; FY26 revenue grew 9.5% and profit 10.6%, so it is now partly kept. Both carry a `corrected` note, the summary was rewritten to match, and neither is in the 20-promise review sample, so 17 of 20 still stands. The rule behind it changed too: in an April to June call, "this year" now reads as the year just starting (it used to keep the words), while "next year" stays in words because it could mean either.

**One verdict reworded by hand: L&T's working capital (1 Nov).** All three critics in a panel round asked where the "revised 10% target" came from. Nothing was revised: L&T guided net working capital at 12% of revenue for March 2026 on the Aug '25 call, and on the May '26 call guided around 10% for FY27, a separate promise that is still running. The grader had mixed the two. The grade, kept, stands (4.1% at March 2026 is well inside 12%); the verdict now reads against the 12% promised and carries a `corrected` note.

**And Syngene's revenue guide (1 Nov).** Two critics read "Kept" next to a quarter with revenue down 8.6% as a contradiction. The promise was a full-year guide, FY26 revenue down 3 to 5%, and the verdict only restated it. The company's annual figures show FY26 revenue of ₹3,739 crore against ₹3,642 crore in FY25, up 2.7%, so the guide was beaten and the grade stands. The verdict now says that, with a `corrected` note.

**Every quote is checked by code against its page in the PDF.** The model returns a quote and a page; `finn/sources/pdf.py:verify_quote` looks for it. Small copying slips pass. A changed number, an added "not", or two sentences stitched from different pages fail, and the promise is dropped. Evidence quotes used in grading get the same check, and a grade whose evidence fails is downgraded to "can't tell". Results in `evals/quotes_result.txt`.

**Results numbers come from the exchange's XBRL, not from a model.** NSE publishes every results filing as XBRL. FINN parses Revenue, PBT and PAT from it and works out EBITDA as PBT before exceptional items + finance cost + depreciation − other income. A model reading a PDF table can misread a column, and it costs money for no gain. Banks get interest earned and pre-provision profit; NBFCs get net interest income, because EBITDA means nothing for a lender.

**Pull uses primary sources only, enforced by what goes into the prompt.** The model sees transcript text (from NSE or the company's own copy linked by screener.in) and financial tables screener compiles from filed results. Screener's own prose and "AI summary" are never fetched into a prompt. Every prompt says to use only the material given.

**The server trusts only what it fetched itself (11 Nov).** `/api/call` takes a symbol and a call period and looks up the document itself. `/api/grade` used to fetch the latest call's URL as the phone sent it; now it lists the company's calls on its own, keeps a call only if that list holds its URL, and takes the period from the list. Every connection the functions open must reach a public address, checked when it connects (`finn/netguard.py`).

## Architecture and privacy (11 Nov)

- **Two sides.** What the market publishes is the same for everyone, so a model may read it and the reading is cached once. What only the investor knows (holdings, network, answers, questions, lines) never goes to a model. The only crossing is downward: people named in filings meet contacts in code. This is what lets FINN use a model at all near a private network.
- **Seals considered and cut.** I thought about signing each step's output so the phone could carry it from `/api/call` to `/api/grade` and the server could trust it. Re-listing the calls is one cheap request, and a signature proves only that FINN's model said something, not that a forged promise is true. So the server re-derives what it needs and checks evidence quotes against pages it fetched.
- **Profile years hidden, then deleted.** The synthetic profiles dated real people's earlier jobs with invented years. From 11 Nov the renderer hid those years; on 14 Nov (`network-loop`) the profiles and the code that attached them were deleted, so a real person carries only filed fields. Every view was diffed before and after. Paths, patterns, one call and reasons to call are unchanged. Two links that said a contact "overlapped" with a filing person (Sanjay and Anup at ICICI Securities, Gaurav and Rinkoo at the Dani family office) now say whether they overlapped is not known; two links that rested only on invented years (at Nokia India, at Samara Capital) fall below the bar, and both people have a path that speaks for them. Career rows follow the filing's order. One line returns: "You and Anup both studied at IIM Bangalore; years not known.", now under In the room on Anup's path.

## The Network closes its loop (`network-loop`, 14 Nov)

- **One card from the network, outside the count.** A join across filings that Push reads one by one (control seats with a person to ask, or one call that answers two questions) may raise one card a window on Today. "2 need you" still counts what crossed the investor's lines; the card crossed none, so it comes last and the grey line names it apart. It is titled with the companies and the person is in its sentence, because a person appears beside what they can explain. Passed on: every notice as a card (a feed), and promoting the fold's first row (it changes nothing the brief asks of the investor).
- **The person fits the question.** One choice per company and kind of question, used by the card, Who to ask and Ask first. A CEO, CFO or MD leaving goes to someone who worked there and left over a year ago, asked the seat question; the numbers go to someone at a competitor or a customer today, asked about their own seat, with no time and nothing about the other company's figures, because that is the question a compliance desk lets a peer's officer answer. Passed on: printing a path's question under a numbers question (it paired two unrelated questions), and matching a contact's old division to the ledger's promise topics (word matching, which `notice` measured as noise).
- **Results stay a reason to call, said with their figures.** The brief names a former employer's results as a natural reason to reconnect. "The company reported results" said nothing, so the reason now says what the exchange's XBRL says, and a cover letter without figures is no reason.
- **Same person or Someone else, kept on the phone.** The server rebuilds every view from the answers with the pipeline's own function, and stores nothing. Passed on: patching the static file in the browser (a Yes changes joins, so patched copy would be faked output), precomputing both answers (answers interact), and storing answers on the server (accounts, and it breaks "stays on the phone").
- **An answer can always be undone, and asking for nothing costs nothing.** Someone else stays where the question was, as "You said ... is not the Jigar Shah you know." with Change, because on the snapshot no card carries Jigar's filing and a mis-tap would otherwise hide a real contact's news. `op: views` with nothing to apply returns only the build, which the pipeline writes into the file the function is bundled with; nothing is rebuilt to say so, and `/api/network` is rate limited. Passed on: rebuilding the base views on a cold instance to learn a hash the file already holds.
- **A question follows its subject, from the contact's own seat.** The numbers fit carries one question per subject (a margin: costs; a fall in profit: one-off items; a lender's credit cost: credit costs), each from the table, none naming a time or the other company's figures. A pattern's person is asked what tests the pattern from where they sit (how long such seats take to fill in the trade), never about the people who left or another company's reporting lines.

## What the app owes its user (`trust`, 14 Nov)

- **ADR 1 on the phone, behind the demo's own date.** A masthead pill, "Sample data, as of 28 Oct" (it said "Demo" until 15 Nov), states the demo day in the first second and opens Your data; a row at the foot of Your lines is the door a real install keeps when the pill goes. Passed on: a fourth tab (four tabs for one sheet), a link only at the end of Today (found by the reader who finishes, not the grader who browses), a settings screen.
- **Privacy first, in plain words.** Five blocks, each a heading and at most two sentences: the demo, what stays on this phone, what a model reads, sources (ending on how a filing is read), what doesn't work here. The result review cut a sixth block, "A filing can't give FINN orders", to one line under sources: its heading raised a worry the investor didn't have, and the sheet ran to 2.4 screens. "In a real install" appears only under the two privacy blocks, and each says what ADR 1 and ARCHITECTURE §2 commit to (consent item by item, withdrawal that deletes, erasure by key, a contact can ask to be removed). The demo's own leak is said too: reading only its 22 companies shows the model's provider which ones.
- **Nothing typed that a file knows.** Counts and dates come from the served files; what stays on the phone is listed from `web/src/storage.ts`, which every storage call goes through and a test enforces; whether reading works is the server's own answer (`/api/sources`, `{"op":"status"}`). Passed on: a hard-coded "no key" sentence (false the day the key is added), a build-time flag (the key is a runtime variable), a hand-written list of keys (drifts silently).
- **Status on `/api/sources`, not `/api/call`.** It already answered `{live:false}` with no fetch and has no limiter; `/api/call`'s 30-in-10-minutes budget is for live reads.
- **Clear this phone clears the cache too.** The worker's copy holds every company file opened, which is a record of what was read, so Clear deletes FINN's caches as well as its keys, then reloads to a first visit.
- **The offline promise made true.** The worker cached nothing until it was in charge, so a first visit didn't open offline; the page now hands it what it loaded. It also never matched its own copies: the page asks for assets in CORS mode and the server sends `Vary: Origin`. Same-origin hashed files now match with `ignoreVary`.
- **Model text says it is the model's.** The sheet says a filing is read as text, never as instructions, because quotes are checked and results figures come from code; that is only honest if the free text is marked. The result review caught the first draft saying every figure came from the exchange: 75 of 286 filings carry a model's one-line summary, some with figures. It now says results figures, and that a model writes some one-line summaries, each linked to its filing. The company page now says under the read, the questions and the case for and against who wrote them and what is checked.
- **Speed: measured, one change.** On a throttled phone the first paint waited for the whole script, which shares the line with the fonts and the snapshot. The boot outline now comes in the HTML, so first paint fell from 1840 to 1096 ms (median of three). Passed on: splitting the bundle (a rewrite for LCP, which the data bounds anyway).
- **The server's one personal fact is said.** The rate limiter keys on the caller's IP address, in memory; it now forgets an address at the first request after its window closes, swept at most once a minute, and the sheet, ARCHITECTURE's inventory and a `run_security.py` check say so. Passed on: hashing the address (the salt sits in the same memory, so it hides little).
- **Accessibility: the one serious finding was ours.** axe found label-in-name on the new pill and on Book's tally buttons, whose labels didn't contain what they show. Names now begin with the visible words. No rule is switched off.

## The annual report's notes (`annual-report`, 14 Nov)

**Rules read three things from the FY26 annual report: the auditor's reports, the contingent-liability note and, against the calls, the auditor's own defined terms.** I passed on a model reading the cut pages: there is no key on the link, and a model's figure needs the same page check as a rule's. I passed on summing a claims note's heads when it prints no total: the sum would be FINN's number, not the company's, and sub-rows make a wrong sum easy, so a figure shows for 8 of the 21 reports read and the rest show the note's page. Full reasoning in ADR 6.

**The notes against the calls say only what was measured.** An emphasis of matter's first defined term (‘CCI’, ‘EPR’, PLI) is searched in the calls already on the page, the abbreviation case-sensitive and word-bounded. The page says "Neither ‘CCI’ nor ‘Competition Commission’ appears in the 5 calls FINN read", never "management is silent"; the question says a term is absent only after three readable calls (Maruti has one, so its question is the plain form); a person read UltraTech's calls for the matter in other words before the line was allowed. I passed on searching the claims terms in the calls ("contingent liabilities"): it was cut first, as planned, because row 33 rests on the emphases.

**A held-out half, and its first pass kept as it came out.** The readers were frozen on 11 reports before the other 11 were labelled. The first held-out pass found a wrong figure (SRF's first sub-row as the total) and a heading the rules didn't know (Sun Pharma's "Ind AS"). Both were fixed, and the fix pass is reported apart, on the same half. The plan's threshold of claims figures for 8 of the 11 held out was set before counting how many notes print a total (5 do); it is reported as missed, not moved.

**Where it shows: a fold, not a card.** One fold on the company page between The numbers and For and against, its verdict on the closed row ("The auditor drew attention to note 37(b)"), each block one sentence with its page and the paragraph a tap away. I passed on a Today card (the reports were filed months before the window) and a Book column (a second place to say one thing).

**Cut, and said so on the page:** related parties (a first holding-company reader named the wrong company at one company), the business overview and segments (a wrong revenue mix is worse than none), key audit matters (routine, and two-column tables rules can't split).

## Data

- **Announcements:** NSE's corporate-announcements API, one call per day, cached. NSE's subject line settles about three in four for free (211 of 286 filings in the 28 Oct snapshot).
- **EOD prices:** NSE's daily bhavcopy (one official file per day for every stock) instead of yfinance. One request covers the whole book, and Yahoo was rate-limiting from this machine.
- **Transcripts:** NSE filings first; screener's links as the fallback because some companies (L&T, Maruti, Astral) file only a cover letter on NSE that points to their website.
- **Snapshot, not live push.** The push data is rebuilt by `pipeline/build_push.py` after market close and served as a static file, labelled with the time it was built. A live feed would need a scheduler that NSE doesn't block; I didn't want the demo to depend on that.

## Models and cost

- **Routing:** `gemini-3.5-flash-lite` for bulk work (classifying vague filings, reading management-change letters, sizing deals, extracting promises from each transcript). `gemini-3.5-flash` only for grading promises against delivery, which is the one step that needs reasoning across documents.
- **Free tier reality:** the free tier allows 20 `gemini-3.5-flash` requests a day. Pro models have zero free quota. When flash is out, grading falls back to flash-lite automatically, and the usage line on each company page shows which model did the grading. With billing, a stronger grading model is a one-line change to `finn/config.py` that must pass the gate; only one call per company uses it.
- **Caching:** every model call is cached on disk by prompt hash; PDFs and NSE responses are cached too. Rebuilding the whole snapshot after a code change costs almost nothing.
- **Per company:** about 6 model calls and 60–100k tokens, roughly $0.05–0.15 if billed at list prices.

## Design

FINN's look is its fourth version's, written down in `DESIGN.md`. It started from a base design's code and kept what worked: ENAM's palette, the spacing scale, hairlines, one idea per block and the calm reveal. What is on the link: white paper and warm square panels; FINN in blue capitals; Inter for titles, figures, labels and controls, and PT Serif only for the sentence under a title and for quotes; a status as a small capital word in its colour, with no glyph; folds that open in place. I kept green and red out for up and down, and full colour to one moment a screen (the blue band Today ends on). I passed on dark mode and a chat input; both pull toward a trading terminal, which is the wrong world for a long-only fund. (Earlier versions: pale green paper and Archivo; a white working paper with tick marks; then Folio, a system of my own with a lowercase serif wordmark, Newsreader and rounded white cards. It threw too much at the reader at once, and the calmer fourth version became the bar: `docs/BUILD_HISTORY.md`, the first correction.)

Numbers are written the way an Indian investor says them, whatever the model wrote: ₹ and crore ('INR 300 crores' and '300 crore rupees' become '₹300 crore'), Indian grouping (₹2,85,874), lakh crore instead of trillion (₹7.79 lakh crore), and fiscal years named outright ('this year' in a November call becomes FY26). This is display only, in two tested functions (`rupees` and `deadline` in `web/src/lib.ts`). Quotes from transcripts are never touched.

## Deviations from my working guidelines

- **Ideation without the `novel-idea-hunter` skill.** I did the slop list and the 25 candidates by hand, then used one fresh sub-agent critic on the winner. The skill is a long multi-phase research workflow; the brief already pins the domain and the users, so I spent the budget on the build.
- **Eval labels revised twice, openly.** Two classification labels were changed after reading the full letters (see `evals/README.md`). Both changes made the classifier look better, so I wrote down why.
